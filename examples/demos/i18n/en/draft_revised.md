# The Keeper

> Skill: `/revise-for-reader` · Input: draft.md + design (reader image & promise) · Revised by the reader's experience: immersion, curiosity, page-turner, promise kept, inducement to reread.

## 1 · The Storm Night

Waves struck the foot of the tower. The keeper stopped wiping the lens and looked out.

Past the dark water, one light swayed. A boat. He stood at the glass. His fingers went white on the frame.

He took his coat and went down the ladder. The wind met him at the door. The sea climbed the rocks and fell back.

He walked to the water's edge. The light out there swayed, held, and then turned away.

He watched until it was gone. Then he climbed back up and shut the door.

The lamp burned. Outside, the water kept on.

## 2 · The Tea

Noon. The sea lay flat. Nothing moved on it.

He filled the kettle and set it on the stove. When it whistled, he poured two cups.

One he set at his place. The other he set across the table.

He drank his own. The second cup cooled.

When the tea was cold, he carried it to the sink and poured it out. He rinsed the cup and set it back on the shelf.

Then he sat again and looked at the water. The chair across the table stayed empty.

## 3 · Dusk

Dusk came. He went down to the beach.

All day no one had come. The sand held a line of footprints, his own, from the morning.

He began at the water's edge and smoothed them away, working back toward the rocks. The tide came up and took what he had smoothed.

When the sand was clear he stopped. He looked out once at the water. Then he climbed to the tower and lit the lamp.

The light turned out over the sea. He stood at the glass and waited.

---

*Revision note: the opening hook of scene 1 is tightened (the light on the water comes sooner), and the scene seams are left unglued — each scene ends on a held image (the water keeping on, the empty chair, the waiting), so the reader is not released until the last line. Nothing is added that names or explains; the promise is kept by not closing.*
