# research-verify — checking (period detail · historical fact)

> Skill: `/research-verify` · Object: design.md (world · character · period setting) · Genre: quiet realist short, an offshore lighthouse, era implied not stated

## What was checked

The story is a keeper by himself on a rock lighthouse, with an oil lamp and a hand-cleaned lens, a supply boat, tea, and a private ritual of smoothing the sand. The era is not fixed to a year, so the check is for internal plausibility within the window the details imply.

## Findings

1. **Light source — accurate.** Before electrification (roughly late 19th to early 20th century), lighthouse lamps burned oil — whale oil, then colza, then kerosene/paraffin. The design's "oil, a wick, a lens he cleans each day" sits inside that window and does not break it.
2. **The lens — accurate.** A Fresnel lens (from the 1820s onward) was cleaned and polished daily; the prisms were dusted and wiped. "Cleans the lens each day" is period-correct and already transmuted into the routine rather than explained.
3. **Lighting at dusk — accurate.** Oil lamps were lit at sunset and extinguished at sunrise, tended through the night. "He lights the lamp at dusk" is correct.
4. **Tea — accurate.** Tea was a staple provision on lightships and lighthouse stations; a kettle and stove are unremarkable. No anachronism.
5. **Supply by sea — accurate.** Offshore rock stations were provisioned by tender/boat at intervals. The supply boat "comes by sea on a schedule the story never states" is correct, and correctly left off the page.

## Corrections reflected

- None of the findings require a change to the draft; the design already avoids the two anachronisms that would break immersion (any electrical light, any modern automated machinery). The check confirms the window and keeps it.
- One guard for the draft: do not let a "bulb", a "switch", or a "motor" slip into the scenes. The lamp is lit with a match and oil; the lens is wiped by hand. This is the level of technology, and the draft holds it.
