# The hook engine (hook-engine)

## ― What makes the reader turn the page: unknown × the want to know ―

"続きが気になる (I have to know what happens next)" is not one of the six reactions. It is the **engine** that all six axes run on. A work may ride any axis — 感嘆 · ユーモア · 感動 · 楽しい · 恐怖 · かわいい — but if the engine dies, the reader leaves. This file expands the one line in [six-response-axes.md](six-response-axes.md) — "Interest is the engine, not an axis" — into the three-part design of a hook.

## 1. The truth of the hook — 未知 × 知りたさ

A hook = **the unknown (未知)** × **the want to know (知りたさ)**. Two terms, both required.

- **unknown** — something the reader does not yet know and cannot predict.
- **the want to know** — the reader *cares* enough to close that gap.

Neither alone is a hook. An unknown the reader does not care about is noise. A want with nothing unknown is satisfaction — and there the story ends.

The two failure modes that split here:

- **器だけ (carrier only)** — an unknown exists, but the reader is not made to want it (the gap isn't attached to anything the reader holds).
- **退屈 (boring)** — everything is predictable; there is no unknown at all.

## 2. The carrier — give, then break (器＝与える→欠けさせる)

The unknown must be planted in something the reader already holds. That thing is the **carrier (器)** — a character, a scene, a special setting. The carrier's charm is the six axes ([six-response-axes.md](six-response-axes.md)); the hook borrows the *open* half of those reactions as fuel (§4).

The move is always the same: **give, then break (与える → 欠けさせる)**.

1. **Give (与える)** — let the reader *possess* something: a bond (絆), an image (像), a promise of trust (信頼). Possession is the precondition of caring.
2. **Break (欠けさせる)** — make it incomplete: take something away, hide something, put it in danger.

The strength of the hook is the strength of the possession. The want to know attaches to what the reader has settled into — the bond, the image, the trusted promise. This attachment is the **transference (乗り移り)**: the reader's want rides on 絆 / 像 / 信頼.

## 3. Placement — the four axes of the chain (配置の4軸)

A hook is not a single moment but a **chain**. The whole-work question is the **trunk (幹)** — the overall hook that must survive to the peak. The scene questions are the **branches (枝)** — small hooks that carry the reader page to page. They interlock along four axes:

| axis | the question it answers | its failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| 継ぎ (continuity) | when an answer lands, does it open the next question? | 断線 (break) — an answer with no next question |
| 進み (progression) | does each branch answer advance the trunk? | 先送り (deferral) — branch answers that never approach the trunk |
| 間合い (spacing) | is the gap scaled to the size of the question? | 遅すぎ / 放置 (too slow / abandoned) — the question is forgotten before it is answered |
| 温存 (preservation) | is the trunk preserved to the peak? | 早すぎ (too early) — the trunk's answer is given at the start (the end of interest) |

A question's lifecycle — 立てる (raise) → 保つ (hold) → 応える (answer) → 継ぐ (hand off) — runs on these four axes. The trunk runs slow, the branches run fast: the gap scales to the size of the question.

## 4. The open half of the six axes (半身)

A hook is a **six-axis reaction, frozen in its open half**. 感嘆's 超出 (exceeding prediction), 感動's 同一化 (identification), 恐怖's 庇護 (protectiveness) — before they *resolve* (納得 · カタルシス · 安心), they *pull* ("why?", "what will happen?", "will they be safe?"). The hook borrows that open, unresolved force and turns its "why?" into the engine. When the reaction finally closes, the open half completes — into the closed reaction the axes name.

## How the skills use this

- **premise** — the trunk: the core question and the promise (信頼経路) are the whole-work hook.
- **plot-design** — the placement: the scene table distributes grab · hold · release, and the trunk's answer is preserved to the peak.
- **下準備 (character-forge / character-bond / worldbuild)** — the carrier: 器 is built here (its charm = the six axes).
- **fast-draft / scene-writer** — the execution: give → break, scene by scene.
- **entertainment** — inspect the engine; fill the weak axis of interest.
- **revise-for-reader / whole-work-review** — check that 継ぎ and 温存 landed; the trunk survives to the end.
